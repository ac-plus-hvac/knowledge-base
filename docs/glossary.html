Glossary — AC Plus Heating & Air

Promotional, educational information about AC Plus Heating & Air. This site is not operated by AC Plus Heating & Air.

  • Split System AC Installation: Split system AC installation, or mini-split installation, is a type of air conditioning installation that mounts a separate indoor air handler and outdoor condenser unit, connects them with refrigerant lines, and wires the system to a dedicated 240V circuit so a building can be cooled without ductwork.
  • Ductless Mini-Split Air Conditioner: A ductless mini-split air conditioner conditions one or more rooms without ductwork.
  • Multi-Zone Mini-Split Installation: Multi-Zone Mini-Split Installation, called zoned ductless AC installation, is a type of air conditioning installation that connects one outdoor condenser unit to multiple indoor air handlers, each cooling or heating a separate room or zone independently, without ductwork, without shared airflow, and without the temperature compromises that come from a single thermostat controlling an entire building.
  • Packaged AC Unit Installation: The compressor, condenser, evaporator coil and air handler.
  • New Construction Air Conditioning Installation: New construction air conditioning installation.
  • AC System Replacement: AC System Replacement, called an Air Conditioner System Changeout, is the full removal of an existing cooling system and the installation of a new one, covering the outdoor condenser unit, the indoor air handler or evaporator coil, refrigerant lines, and the electrical connections that tie everything together.
  • AC Unit Upgrade Installation: AC Unit Upgrade Installation, called AC System Replacement, is a full-scope HVAC service that removes an existing air conditioner and installs a new, higher-capacity or higher-efficiency unit in its place, covering equipment removal and disposal, new condenser mounting, refrigerant line connection, electrical disconnect work, ductwork integration and thermostat configuration.
  • Air Conditioning System Sizing: Air Conditioning System Sizing.
  • Evaporator Coil: An evaporator coil, called an A-coil or cooling coil, is a heat exchanger installed inside an air conditioning system.
  • Condensate Drain System: A condensate drain system.
  • Condensate Drain Line: A condensate drain line.
  • Concrete Equipment Pad: A concrete equipment pad.
  • Electrical Disconnect Switch: An electrical disconnect switch, called an AC disconnect box, is a type of electrical safety equipment that manually cuts off all power to an air conditioning unit.
  • R-32 Refrigerant: R-32 Refrigerant, known as HFC-32 or difluoromethane, is a single-component hydrofluorocarbon refrigerant with the chemical formula CH2F2, a molecular weight of 52.02 g/mol, a boiling point of -51.7°C at standard atmospheric pressure, a Global Warming Potential of 675, and a flammability classification of A2L, making it one of the more widely adopted modern refrigerants in residential and commercial air conditioning systems across the United States and globally.
  • HVAC Ductwork: HVAC ductwork, called ducting, is a network of air distribution channels that carries conditioned air from a heating or cooling unit to every room in a building, including supply ducts that push treated air out, return ducts that pull stale air back, plenums, fittings, and vents.
  • Ductwork Installation: Ductwork installation, called ducting installation, is the fabrication and fitting of an air distribution system within a building structure.
  • Air Distribution System: An air distribution system, or duct system, is a network of ducts, vents, registers, dampers and plenums that moves conditioned air from a central heating or cooling unit to every room in a building and returns stale air back to the unit for reconditioning.
  • Air Vents: Air vents, called air registers or diffusers, are openings fitted with grilles, louvers, damper blades and mounting frames that allow conditioned air to enter a room, control the direction of airflow and regulate how much air each space receives.
  • HVAC Zoning System: An HVAC zoning system, called a zoned HVAC system, divides a building into independent temperature-controlled areas.
  • Equipment Placement: Equipment Placement, called Unit Siting, is the deliberate process of positioning AC units at specified locations within a structure, covering indoor unit height, outdoor condenser clearance, mount surface load-bearing capacity, orientation relative to sun and airflow, and the distance from walls, obstructions and heat sources.
  • Insulation: Insulation, called thermal insulation, is a building material designed to slow heat transfer between spaces.
  • Inadequate Cooling Capacity: Inadequate cooling capacity, called poor cooling performance, is a condition where an air conditioning unit's BTU output falls short of the space's actual cooling load, leaving rooms warmer than the thermostat setpoint regardless of how long the system runs.
  • Inefficient Cooling: Inefficient cooling, called low cooling efficiency, is a measurable condition in which an air conditioning system fails to deliver its rated cooling output at expected energy consumption levels, producing symptoms that include a temperature differential greater than 2°F from the thermostat setpoint, a SEER value below the unit's rated figure, elevated kWh draw, slower-than-normal time to reach the desired temperature and a BTU/hr output that falls short of the system's nameplate capacity.
  • Uneven Indoor Temperatures: Uneven indoor temperatures, called HVAC imbalance, are an Indoor Air Comfort Problem defined by inconsistent air temperature distribution across different rooms or zones within a building.
  • High Utility Bills: High utility bills represent a measurable Energy Cost Problem.
  • High Energy Bills: High energy bills, or elevated utility costs, are a measurable financial problem caused by excessive electrical consumption from HVAC equipment, particularly air conditioning systems running in hot climates like the Mojave Desert, where cooling loads push monthly consumption well above baseline for weeks at a time.
  • End-of-Life HVAC System: An end-of-life HVAC system has exceeded its functional service lifespan.
  • Obsolete AC System: An obsolete AC system is an air conditioning system that no longer meets current efficiency, refrigerant, or building code standards.
  • Failed Compressor: A failed compressor, called compressor burnout, is a type of AC component failure in which the compressor can no longer pressurize refrigerant through the cooling cycle, causing the entire air conditioning system to stop producing cold air.
  • R-22 Phase-Out: The R-22 Phase-Out, formally known as the HCFC-22 Regulatory Ban, is a U.S.
  • Duct Sealing: Duct sealing, called HVAC Duct Sealing, is a type of HVAC System Efficiency Service that closes gaps, cracks and disconnected joints in a home's ductwork to stop conditioned air from escaping before it reaches the living space.
  • Airflow Testing: Airflow Testing, called Ductwork Airflow Assessment, is a diagnostic process that measures air movement volume and velocity through an HVAC system.
  • Noise Level Considerations: Noise Level Considerations, called Sound Output or AC Sound Assessment, are the evaluation and management of sound produced by air conditioning equipment to keep decibel levels within acceptable ranges for occupied spaces, covering compressor vibration, indoor-to-outdoor unit proximity, frequency range of emitted sound, distance-dependent noise measurement and the placement decisions that follow from each reading.
  • Old AC Unit Removal: Old AC unit removal, called old air conditioner removal, is the disconnection, extraction, and disposal of an existing air conditioning unit before a new system goes in.
  • System Startup and Commissioning: System Startup and Commissioning, called AC commissioning, is the structured verification process that confirms a newly installed or replaced air conditioning system operates to its design specifications.
  • Refrigerant Management Regulations: Refrigerant Management Regulations, called F-Gas Regulations, are a type of Environmental Protection Regulations that govern the purchase, handling, recovery, reclamation and disposal of refrigerants used in HVAC systems, covering services from technician certification requirements and leak rate thresholds to GWP limits, reporting protocols and penalty provisions for non-compliance.
  • Installation Safety Measures: Installation safety measures, called Workplace Safety Procedures, are mandatory procedural controls applied during AC installation to protect workers and building occupants from electrical hazards, refrigerant exposure, falls and structural damage.
  • HVAC Safety Standards: HVAC Safety Standards, called Mechanical Safety Regulations, are codified rules specifying the safe design, installation, inspection, and operation of heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems.
  • Building Codes Compliance: Building Codes Compliance, called regulatory adherence, is a type of Regulatory Compliance that governs how HVAC equipment is selected, installed, inspected and approved across residential and commercial properties, covering jurisdiction-specific code versions, mechanical permit requirements, load calculation documentation, equipment clearance specifications, inspection records and approval certifications.
  • Permit Pull: Permit Pull, called the Permitting Process, is a type of administrative process in which a homeowner or licensed contractor submits an application to a local government authority to obtain official authorization before beginning construction, installation, or replacement work on a heating and cooling system, including central air conditioning systems, ductless mini split systems, heat pumps and related mechanical equipment.
  • HVAC Installation Permits: HVAC installation permits.
  • California Building Energy Efficiency Standards: California Building Energy Efficiency Standards, known as the California Energy Code, are the mandatory statewide regulations that set minimum energy performance requirements for residential and nonresidential buildings, covering HVAC equipment efficiency ratings, building envelope thermal performance, insulation R-values by climate zone, lighting standards and mechanical ventilation requirements.
  • CSLB License: A CSLB License, formally called a California Contractor License, is a state-issued credential granted by the Contractors State License Board that authorizes individuals and companies to legally perform construction and specialty trade work in California, including HVAC installation, general building, plumbing, electrical, and dozens of other classifications.
  • Manufacturer Warranty Registration: Manufacturer Warranty Registration, called Product Warranty Registration, is a formal administrative process in which a homeowner or installing contractor records a new equipment purchase with the manufacturer.
  • Rebates and Incentives: Rebates and incentives, called energy efficiency incentives or discounts, are financial reductions and credits offered by utility companies, equipment manufacturers, and government agencies to lower the net cost of qualifying HVAC installations, including central air conditioning systems, ductless mini split systems and heat pumps.
  • Utility Rebate Programs: Utility Rebate Programs, called Energy Rebates, are utility-funded cash-back programs that reward residential and commercial customers who install qualifying high-efficiency HVAC equipment, including central air conditioning systems, ductless mini split systems, heat pumps and other energy-efficient cooling and heating units.
  • Professional Installation Process: A defined installation workflow is a sequence of technical steps that licensed HVAC technicians typically follow to site-assess, mount, connect, and commission an air conditioning system so it can operate at full capacity from day one.
  • HERS Rating: HERS Rating.
  • HERS Verification Test: The HERS Verification Test.
  • Cost Estimation: Cost estimation, called a Service Estimate or Installation Quote, is a structured calculation of projected expenses completed before AC installation work begins, covering labor costs, equipment pricing, site survey evaluation, material costs and overhead allocation so a homeowner knows the full financial scope before any work starts.
  • AHRI Certified Equipment: AHRI Certified Equipment, known as AHRI verified equipment, is a type of HVAC Equipment Certification in which cooling and heating units are independently tested and verified by the Air-Conditioning, Heating, and Refrigeration Institute to confirm that published performance ratings match real-world output, covering rated capacity in BTU/hr, Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio (SEER2), Energy Efficiency Ratio (EER2), Heating Seasonal Performance Factor (HSPF2) and other efficiency metrics across central air conditioning systems, ductless mini split systems, heat pumps and packaged units.
  • Central Air Conditioner Repair: Central air conditioner repair, called Ducted Air Conditioning Service or Whole-House AC Repair, is the service of diagnosing and fixing malfunctions in central AC units, covering the compressor, refrigerant circuit, air handler, electrical controls, condensate drain, evaporator coil and the ductwork that distributes conditioned air through the home.
  • AC Diagnostic Inspection: An AC Diagnostic Inspection, called an air conditioning diagnostic check, is a structured evaluation of a cooling system that identifies faults, performance deficiencies and component failures across refrigerant circuits, electrical controls, thermostat calibration, airflow paths and mechanical parts.
  • Ductless Mini-Split Repair: Ductless mini-split repair, called mini-split service or split-system repair service, is the diagnosis and correction of malfunctions in a ductless HVAC system, covering the indoor air handler, the outdoor compressor and condenser unit, the refrigerant line set, electrical connections, control boards and drainage components.
  • Heat Pump Cooling System Repair: Heat Pump Cooling System Repair, called Reverse-Cycle Cooling Repair, is the diagnosis and correction of faults in a heat pump's cooling function, covering refrigerant leaks, reversing valve failures, compressor performance loss, electrical faults, coil contamination and control-board malfunctions that prevent the system from moving heat out of a conditioned space.
  • Package Unit Repair: Package unit repair, called Rooftop Unit Repair, is the diagnosis and restoration of a self-contained all-in-one heating and cooling unit.
  • Run Capacitor: A run capacitor.
  • Start Capacitor: A start capacitor, called a Motor Start Capacitor, is a type of electrical capacitor that delivers a short, high-energy current boost to an AC motor at the moment it starts, providing the phase-shifted torque needed to overcome the motor's initial inertia and bring it up to operating speed.
  • AC Contactor: An AC contactor is an electromechanical switching device.
  • Contactor Failure: Contactor failure.
  • Condenser Fan Motor Failure: Condenser fan motor failure, called outdoor fan motor failure, is a type of air conditioning problem in which the motor driving the outdoor unit's fan stops working, halting airflow across the condenser coil and preventing the system from shedding heat.
  • AC Circuit Board Failure: AC Circuit Board Failure.
  • Thermostat Malfunction: Thermostat malfunction, called faulty thermostat behavior, is a failure condition where the thermostat cannot accurately read or signal temperature control commands to the heating and cooling system.
  • Expansion Valve: An expansion valve, called a TXV (thermostatic expansion valve), is a refrigerant metering device that reduces high-pressure liquid refrigerant to a low-pressure state and controls exactly how much refrigerant enters the evaporator coil, making it one of the four core components in every refrigeration cycle alongside the compressor, condenser and evaporator.
  • AC Filter: An AC filter, called a furnace filter or HVAC filter, is a replaceable media barrier that removes airborne particles from the air stream entering an air conditioning system, capturing dust, pollen, pet dander, mold spores and other particulates before they reach the blower, evaporator coil and ductwork.
  • AC Drain Pan: An AC drain pan, called a condensate drain pan or drip pan, is a shallow basin mounted beneath the evaporator coil of an air handler that collects the condensation water the coil produces during normal cooling operation, channels it toward a drain port, and prevents that moisture from reaching the surrounding structure, electrical components and flooring.
  • Refrigerant Line Set: A refrigerant line set, called a copper lineset, is a paired copper tubing assembly that carries refrigerant between the indoor evaporator coil and the outdoor condenser unit of a split air conditioning system.
  • AC Not Blowing Cold Air: AC Not Blowing Cold Air, called insufficient cooling, is a failure state where an air conditioning unit runs but delivers air that is not adequately cooled, leaving supply air temperature more than 2°F above the thermostat setpoint, with measurable drops in airflow volume at vents, abnormal refrigerant pressure readings on the suction and discharge lines, and a compressor that may be cycling on and off or failing to engage at all.
  • Low AC Airflow: Low AC airflow, called Restricted AC Airflow, is a measurable AC system performance problem.
  • AC Leaking Water: AC leaking water is called an HVAC water leak or condensate overflow.
  • AC Freezing Up: AC freezing up, called evaporator coil icing, is a type of Air Conditioner Malfunction in which ice forms on the evaporator coil surface, blocks airflow through the system and prevents the unit from transferring heat out of the building.
  • Noisy AC Unit: A noisy AC unit, or loud air conditioner, is a type of AC System Malfunction in which the air conditioning system emits unwanted sounds during operation, including rattling, grinding, buzzing, humming, banging, squealing and clicking, each pointing to a different mechanical or electrical fault inside the system.
  • Foul AC Odors: Foul AC odors, called HVAC odor emissions, are unpleasant smells produced by an air conditioning unit due to biological growth, chemical faults, or mechanical failures inside the system, including mold spore off-gassing from the evaporator coil, burnt electrical insulation from failing components, stagnant water in the condensate drain, refrigerant leaks from cracked lines and refrigerant leaks from cracked lines and fittings, and debris accumulation inside the ductwork.
  • Electrical Faults in AC System: Electrical faults in AC system.
  • AC Coil Cleaning Service: AC Coil Cleaning Service, called AC coil maintenance, is the process of removing accumulated dirt, dust, debris and biological buildup from an air conditioner's evaporator and condenser coils so the system transfers heat the way it was designed to.
  • AC Electrical System Troubleshooting: AC electrical system troubleshooting, called HVAC electrical fault finding, is a diagnostic process that identifies faults in an AC unit's electrical circuits and components, including capacitors, contactors, control boards, wiring harnesses, voltage supply lines and low-voltage control circuits.
  • R-22 Refrigerant Phase-Out: The R-22 Refrigerant Phase-Out, called the Freon Phase-Out, is an EPA-mandated regulatory process that permanently ended the production and import of R-22 refrigerant in the United States.
  • EPA Refrigerant Handling Regulations: EPA Refrigerant Handling Regulations, called Refrigerant Management Rules, are a set of federal environmental compliance standards under the Clean Air Act that govern how refrigerants must be recovered, recycled, reclaimed and disposed of during the service, maintenance and decommissioning of cooling and refrigeration equipment.
  • AC Repair Service Warranty: An AC Repair Service Warranty, called a service assurance plan, is a contractual commitment that covers defects in labor, parts, or both for a defined period following a completed air conditioning repair, protecting homeowners in Victorville from repeat failures on the same component or workmanship issue.
  • AC Parts Manufacturer Warranty: An AC Parts Manufacturer Warranty, called an OEM Parts Warranty, is a guarantee issued by the original equipment manufacturer.
  • California Section 608 Refrigerant Certification: California Section 608 Refrigerant Certification.
  • CSLB HVAC Contractor License: A CSLB HVAC Contractor License, called a California HVAC Contractor License, is a state-issued Contractor Licensing Credential that authorizes a contractor to legally install, repair and maintain heating, ventilating and air-conditioning systems in California, covering residential split systems, commercial rooftop units, ductless mini splits, heat pumps and related equipment.
  • High Desert Climate Heat Stress: High Desert Climate Heat Stress, called Arid Zone Thermal Stress, is an environmental condition in which extreme dry heat, intense solar radiation and critically low atmospheric moisture combine to impose sustained mechanical strain on cooling systems, building envelopes, electrical components and any equipment that depends on ambient air for heat rejection.
  • Central Air Conditioner Replacement: Central Air Conditioner Replacement, called a Central AC Upgrade, typically involves removing an existing central cooling system and installing a new one, which can cover the outdoor condenser unit, indoor evaporator coil, refrigerant lines, electrical connections and a system test before the home is returned to service.
  • Evaporative Cooler to Central AC Conversion: Evaporative cooler to central AC conversion, sometimes called an Evaporative to Refrigerant AC Retrofit, typically replaces a swamp cooler system with a ducted central air conditioning system that cools indoor air through refrigerant-based heat exchange rather than water evaporation, and the work can include removing the existing evaporative unit, installing a new split-system AC, fabricating or modifying ductwork, making electrical upgrades and integrating a thermostat.
  • Matched System Replacement: Matched System Replacement, called a Matched HVAC System Install, is the full replacement of a home's cooling system using an indoor unit and outdoor condensing unit that are factory-rated and tested together, covering the air handler or furnace, the evaporator coil and the outdoor unit as a single engineered set.
  • Split System Air Conditioner Replacement: Split system air conditioner replacement, called a split system change-out, is the removal of an existing split system unit and the installation of a new one in its place, covering the outdoor condenser, the indoor air handler or evaporator coil, the refrigerant lines, the electrical connections and the thermostat wiring.
  • Heat Pump Air Conditioner Replacement: Heat pump air conditioner replacement is the removal of an existing cooling-only system and the installation of a heat pump unit that handles both heating and cooling from a single piece of equipment, covering the outdoor condenser, the indoor air handler, the refrigerant lineset, electrical connections and a full system test before the job is done.
  • Packaged Air Conditioner Replacement: Packaged air conditioner replacement.
  • Single Stage Air Conditioner: A single stage air conditioner, called a 1-Stage AC, is a central cooling system whose compressor runs at one fixed speed and switches fully on or fully off depending on whether the thermostat calls for cooling.
  • Two-Stage Air Conditioner: A two-stage air conditioner, called a dual-stage AC, is a type of air conditioner with a compressor that runs at two distinct capacity levels.
  • Variable Speed Air Conditioner: A variable speed air conditioner, called an inverter AC, is a type of air conditioner that continuously adjusts its compressor speed to match the exact cooling demand of a space, rather than switching fully on or fully off the way older systems do.
  • AC Condensate Drainage System: An AC condensate drainage system, called an AC drain line or condensate drain, is a network of pipes, trays and mechanical components that collects moisture pulled from indoor air during the cooling process and routes that water safely out of the building.
  • Ductwork Modification: Ductwork modification, called duct reconfiguration, is the alteration of existing ductwork to accommodate a new or replacement air conditioning system.
  • AC Evaporator Coil Leak: An AC evaporator coil leak, called an AC Freon leak, is a breach in the sealed refrigerant circuit at the indoor evaporator coil that allows refrigerant to escape, drops system pressure, reduces heat absorption, and causes the air conditioner to lose its ability to cool the space it serves.
  • Inefficient AC System: An inefficient AC system.
  • Frequent AC Breakdowns: Frequent AC breakdowns, called persistent AC issues, are recurrent failures of an air conditioning system that require repair or intervention more than three times in a single year, covering compressor faults, refrigerant leaks, electrical failures, frozen coils, drainage problems and sensor malfunctions that prevent the system from maintaining a set indoor temperature.
  • Uneven Home Cooling: Uneven home cooling, called temperature imbalance, is an HVAC performance issue where certain rooms in a home are consistently warmer or cooler than others, creating measurable differences in comfort, airflow and system runtime across zones.
  • No AC Cooling: No AC cooling, called no cooling, is a complete failure of an air conditioning system to remove heat from indoor air, leaving supply air at or near room temperature regardless of thermostat setting, compressor runtime, or fan speed.
  • AC System Beyond Repair: An AC system beyond repair, called an unrepairable AC unit, is a heating and cooling condition where the cost, scope or physical state of the damage makes restoration economically or practically impossible, covering compressor seizure, cracked heat exchangers, failed evaporator coils, corroded refrigerant lines, burned electrical boards and multiple simultaneous component failures that together exceed the value of the equipment itself.
  • Electrical Upgrade for AC: Electrical upgrade for AC, called an AC Electrical Wiring Upgrade, is modification of existing electrical infrastructure to support a new or replacement air conditioning unit, covering panel capacity increases, dedicated circuit installation, breaker replacement, wiring gauge changes and voltage supply corrections.
  • Local Building Codes: Local building codes, called HVAC codes or building regulations, are legally binding rules governing how heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems are designed, installed, replaced, and inspected within a specific jurisdiction.
  • Refrigerant Handling Regulations: Refrigerant Handling Regulations, known as Section 608, are federal environmental rules governing the proper management, recovery, recycling, and disposal of refrigerants.
  • Utility Rebates for AC Replacement: Utility rebates for AC replacement, called Energy Rebates or AC Incentives, are financial incentive programs offered by utility companies and government agencies that reduce the out-of-pocket cost of replacing an old, inefficient air conditioning system with a qualifying high-efficiency unit.
  • HVAC Equipment Warranty: An HVAC equipment warranty, called a Manufacturer Warranty or Manufacturer's Guarantee, is a formal guarantee issued by the equipment manufacturer that covers repair or replacement of system components that fail due to defects in materials or workmanship, including compressors, heat exchangers, coils, circuit boards, motors and other factory-installed parts.
  • 10 Year Compressor Warranty: A 10 Year Compressor Warranty, called an Extended Compressor Guarantee, is a manufacturer's guarantee covering repair or replacement of an AC compressor for a full decade from the date of installation.
  • HVAC Labor Warranty: An HVAC labor warranty, called a Workmanship Guarantee, is a contractual promise by an HVAC contractor to perform repair labor at no charge for a defined period after installation.
  • Properly Sized AC System: A properly sized AC system, called a right-sized AC unit, is an air conditioning system whose cooling capacity is matched precisely to the calculated heat load of the building it serves, accounting for square footage, insulation levels, window area, ceiling height, local climate data and internal heat sources.
  • Manual J Load Calculation: Manual J Load Calculation, called ACCA Manual J Calculation, is a standardized engineering procedure that determines how much heating and cooling a building actually needs by measuring the building envelope, climate zone, internal heat sources, window area, insulation levels, duct layout and occupancy together in one calculation.
  • System Commissioning: System Commissioning, called HVAC Commissioning, is the final verification and adjustment process that confirms an air conditioning system operates exactly as designed before the homeowner relies on it for daily comfort.
  • Duct Leakage Testing: Duct leakage testing, called a duct tightness test, measures how much conditioned air escapes through gaps, cracks, and poorly sealed joints in an HVAC ductwork system before it reaches the living space.
  • Static Pressure Testing: Static pressure testing, called a Ductwork Pressure Test or Total Static Pressure Test, is a diagnostic measurement that quantifies how much resistance air encounters as it moves through an HVAC system's duct network, supply plenum, return plenum and air handler.
  • Manufacturer Equipment Warranty: A manufacturer equipment warranty, called an OEM Warranty or Factory Warranty, is a contractual guarantee issued by the equipment maker that covers defects in materials and workmanship across the compressor, heat exchanger, coil, blower motor, control board and other factory-installed components of a residential or commercial cooling system.
